Some things I believe:
  • Just do it, and then do more
    • The time spent arguing over who owns a task almost always exceeds the time the task would have taken
    • "That's not my scope" is actually a choice to avoid ownership
    • Take ownership, figure it out, do the damn job
  • Just do cool things
    • "Cool" is a better filter than "strategic" — it tracks hard, useful, and not-done-yet all at once
    • Nobody remembers the safe project
    • If you can't explain why it's cool, you probably don't actually want to build it
  • Make contact with reality as early as you can
    • A simulation is a hypothesis; a populated board is evidence
    • Almost everything I've gotten wrong was something I never modeled
    • Boards are cheap and fast now — there's rarely a good reason not to spin one
  • Go wide before you go deep
    • The best idea usually comes from the field next door
    • Most "original" work is a transplant
    • Being a beginner on purpose keeps you honest
  • Attention is the scarce input, not time
    • Four good hours beat twelve mediocre ones
    • Protect the mornings; everything else can move
    • Every notification is someone else's priority
  • Write it down
    • Writing is how you find out what you actually think
    • A decision you can't explain in a paragraph isn't made yet
    • Your notes are a letter to someone who forgot everything: you, in two years
  • Finishing is a separate skill from starting, and the rarer one
    • Starting is cheap. The last ten percent is where almost everyone quits
    • A finished mediocre thing teaches you more than an unfinished great one
    • Nothing is real until someone who isn't you has used it
  • Put yourself where luck can find you
    • Luck scales with surface area
    • Say yes to anything cheap to try with an uncapped upside
    • Most of what went well for me traces back to something small I nearly skipped
  • Prefer being corrected to being right
    • Wrong early is cheap; wrong late is expensive
    • Find the people who'll tell you the truth, then make it easy for them
    • If nobody is pushing back, you're in the wrong room
  • Understand one layer below where you work
    • If you write firmware, read the schematic; if you draw schematics, know the physics
    • Abstractions are great until they leak, and they always leak
    • The bugs that cost weeks live at the boundaries between layers
  • You don't really know something until you've taught it
    • An eight-year-old will ask the question your professor didn't
    • If you can't explain it simply, that's a problem with your model, not your vocabulary
